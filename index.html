Wonjun Jo

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Electrical Engineering at POSTECH. I conduct my research at the Advanced Machine Intelligence (AMI) Lab at KAIST, advised by Prof. Tae-Hyun Oh.

My research focuses on learning and adaptation for embodied agents. Building on self-supervised and multimodal learning, I study how robots can acquire new capabilities while preserving and generalizing what they already know.

My current work explores the adaptation of pretrained robot policies and the acquisition of reusable skills from demonstrations and interaction. I am particularly interested in how embodied agents can learn from failures and transfer their skills to new tasks and environments.

VISION & JOURNEY

From a distant goal
to the work I do today.

My long-term goal is to help build embodied AI agents that carry out human goals autonomously, robustly, and across unfamiliar tasks and environments — without continuous teleoperation.

I hope to see these robots become useful in human society, then work where human access is difficult or costly. Ultimately, I want to contribute to robots that help build the first habitats in space and on Mars, supporting human settlement and, in the longer term, terraforming.

Where I am now

Building on robust perception, I am working on robot policy adaptation and reusable agentic skills, alongside modular Physical AI research at KIST.

THE LONG-TERM GOAL

Work beyond easy human reach.

Robots that take high-level goals and independently perform useful work in remote, extreme, or extraterrestrial environments.

My ultimate aspiration is to contribute to the construction and maintenance of initial habitats for human settlement, including on Mars.

What lies ahead

Extended autonomy, recovery from unexpected failures, reliable hardware, and validation under the conditions of a real mission.

This is the destination I hope to work toward; my current research is in robot learning and perception.

A DEPLOYMENT GOAL

Become useful in everyday life.

I want embodied agents to perform tasks people actually need, in environments that are not arranged solely for a demonstration.

What I still need to work toward

Safe operation around people, understandable behavior, dependable task completion, and learning what makes a robot genuinely useful to its users.

How my current work connects

Cooking is a human-relevant setting in the KIST project. Policy adaptation and skill reuse address parts of the flexibility I want such robots to have.

Real-world deployment and safety validation remain future work.
